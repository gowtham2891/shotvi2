# Telugu short-form playbook: cutting Reels and Shorts from podcasts

Owner: `telugu-content-strategist`. Version 1.0 (2026-09-27).
Audience: everyone who writes selection logic, prompts, or judges clips. Scoring anchors are in
`labeling_guide.md`; hard rules and numbers are in `docs/spec/clip_spec.md`. This doc is the *why* behind them.

Examples are quoted from the two dev episodes (a film-star interview and a fitness/nutrition podcast) because
they're the transcripts we have, but every pattern below holds across genres: film, health, business,
spirituality, comedy, politics, personal stories. Where a pattern is genre-specific I say so.

---

## 1. What a Telugu podcast short is actually competing with

The viewer is thumb-scrolling Reels/Shorts between movie edits, meme pages and news clips. They give us about
**1–3 seconds** to show a reason to stay, and about **20–40 seconds** before they want the point. They watch
with the sound on more often than not, but read the burnt-in captions too. They don't know the guest's full story
and won't go looking for it. So a podcast clip wins only if it feels like a complete mini-video: a hook, just
enough context, a turn, and an ending that pays.

Telugu audiences in particular reward:
- **Relatability over polish.** "నాకు అందరిలాగే morning లేవగానే coffee, TV… exercise వద్దు అనిపిస్తుంది" (a 60-year-old
  action star) beats a perfect fitness tip. "Chicken మిగిలితే కొడుకు తింటే చాలు, నేను పచ్చడి అన్నం తింటా" gets shared to
  family groups because every viewer knows that amma.
- **Mass and attitude.** A crisp dialogue with swagger, a self-aware brag, a "పోరా తేరికి". The line people quote in
  comments.
- **Inside stories and money figures.** "12 మంది చూశారు… next day bag తో వచ్చారు", "celebrity coach కి నెలకి 8 lakhs",
  "Mani Ratnam గారే నన్ను villain చేశారు". Telugu viewers love "అసలు ఏం జరిగింది" content.
- **Myth vs fact on everyday life.** Food, money, health, family customs: "నాటు కోడి vs broiler", "whey protein
  తాగితే పిల్లలు పుట్టరా?", "బిర్యానీ unhealthy నా?". Anything that argues with what amma or the WhatsApp uncle says.
- **Emotion without melodrama.** A guest going quiet ("I remember only the pain behind that"), a late sister's
  nudge, a mother saying "నేనున్నాను". Understated beats land harder than crying.
- **Tenglish as it's spoken.** Code-mixing is not noise; it's the register. Captions and titles should keep it.

## 2. Moments that land (find these first)

An editor scrubs for **moments**, not topics. These are the moment types that reliably become good shorts, with
the signal to look for in a transcript.

| Moment | What it sounds like | Transcript signal | Example from the dev set |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bold claim / hot take** | A sentence that could be a caption and that people will agree or fight with. | Short declarative line, often a generalisation ("ఈ సమాజం…", "ఎవడూ…", "అసలు choice కాదు"). | "ఈ సమాజం they understand only two languages: success and money" · "ఆడవాళ్ళకి exercise choice కాదు, mandate" |
| **Myth vs fact** | Host states a common belief; guest overturns it with one reason. | Host question with "అంటారు కదా", "correct ఏనా?", "myth ఏనా?"; answer starts "లేదు" / "See". | "మా parents కి 40 వరకు sugar లేదు" → "వాళ్ళు 40 వరకు test చేయించలేదు" · Diet coke, mutton & cancer, whey protein |
| **Rock bottom → comeback** | Loss, humiliation, then the turn. The turn must be inside the clip. | Words like "zero అయిపోయాను", "ఎవరూ రాలేదు", "phone ఎత్తలేదు" followed later by "then…", "next day". | Arjun sells his only property, 12 people praise the second half, buyers arrive with a bag |
| **Vivid personal incident** | A specific scene with dialogue, place, people. The guest acts it out. | Quoted speech inside the answer, place names, numbers, laughter events nearby. | Railway-station fan who wouldn't let go; Nellore crowd pulling the Ambassador doors; 3 days on the floor with a slipped disc |
| **Celebrity anecdote** | A known name plus something unseen. | Star names in the answer, not just the question. | Chiranjeevi calling from dubbing; Soundarya crying because Arjun didn't win; Nani's brief for Paradise |
| **Money / numbers reveal** | Salaries, fees, costs, kilos, years. Specific numbers make it believable and shareable. | Digits and "lakhs", "per month", "kgs", "years". | "₹17,800 జీతం", "₹25,000 competition", "1–8 lakhs per month", "180 టన్నులు, 17 ఏళ్ళు" |
| **Practical tip with a rule** | A usable instruction with a number or a simple test. | Imperatives ("కొనకండి", "పెట్టుకోండి"), numbers, "simple గా చెప్పాలంటే". | "Diet coke రోజుకి ఒకటి max" · "sets మధ్య 2–3 నిమిషాలు" · "30+ అయితే ఈ tests చేయించండి" |
| **Relatable confession** | A star or expert admitting a normal weakness. | "నాకు కూడా…", "నేను కూడా…", "అందరిలాగే". | Arjun: coffee and TV instead of gym; coach: earned less than his girlfriend |
| **Self-deprecating humour / act-out** | The speaker mimics others or himself. | Quoted voices, laughter events right after. | Arjun mimicking fans: "అప్పట్లో బాగుండేవాడులే, ఇప్పుడేం లేడు" · "Ego lifting: అమ్మాయి కనపడితే రెండు plates" |
| **Quotable one-liner** | A line that works as a WhatsApp status. | Parallel structure, English-Telugu mix, often repeated by the speaker. | "Take care of your body for one hour, it takes care of you for 23" · "costly రస్నా" · "ego ని gym బయట వదిలేసి రా" |
| **Emotional beat** | Quiet, heavy, specific. | Pauses (gaps > 1s) in the middle of a story, words like "pain", "passed away", "అమ్మ". | "నేనున్నాను, ఏదైనా అమ్మిస్తాను – మా mother చెప్పింది" · "I remember only the pain behind that" |
| **Host's sharp question** | Sometimes the host's line is the hook. | A question that names a controversy or a celebrity. | "Mutton తింటే cancer వస్తది?" · "Whey protein తీసుకుంటే పిల్లలు పుట్టరని అంటారు" |

Genre notes:
- **Film/celebrity:** anecdotes, fan incidents, money and industry reveals, mimicry, and "why I said yes/no to
  that role". Punchlines are often dialogues in the guest's screen voice.
- **Health/fitness/finance/"expert" podcasts:** myth-busting and numbers do the work. The best clips pair a
  common belief with a simple mechanism and a rule. Every such clip carries a health/finance flag for the creator.
- **Spirituality/devotional:** a crisp teaching line, a story with a moral, a surprising fact about a temple or
  ritual (e.g. "32 ft monolithic ఆంజనేయస్వామి, 180 టన్నులు, 17 ఏళ్ళు"). Payoff is the moral, stated plainly.
- **Politics/news:** strong claims and exchanges land, but so does risk. Flag named allegations; keep the full
  claim and its qualifier together.
- **Comedy/entertainment:** set-up and punch, often two speakers. Keep the laugh gap after the punch; cut
  explanations of the joke.
- **Personal/career stories:** a specific number (salary, age, debt) and a decision point make them work.

## 3. How regional editors cut (and how Shotvi should)

### 3.1 Find the moment, then set the ends
1. **Find the payoff first.** The line people will quote, the reveal, the laugh.
2. **Walk back to the minimum setup** the payoff needs: the question it answers, the name behind "ఆయన", the
   situation. Often that's the host's question; sometimes one earlier sentence of the guest.
3. **Start on the hook**: the first line that already makes sense *and* pulls. If the setup is dull, consider a
   cold open (3.4).
4. **End on the payoff.** One beat after the punchline at most (a laugh, "అంతే", a short tag like "అని train ఎక్కి
   వెళ్ళిపోయాం"). Never the wrap-up paragraph that follows.

### 3.2 Tighten like an editor
Editors typically keep about half of the source span. What goes:
- **Listener backchannels** in the main speaker's flow: "ఉమ్", "మ్", "అవును", "Right", "Okay", "makes sense",
  "ఆహా". They're split into their own sentences in Scribe, so they're easy to drop. Keep one only when it's a
  reaction at a punchline (a laugh, "బాబో!").
- **False starts and stutters** when they don't carry emotion: "ఏ-ఏమి", "o-only", "ఎవ్-ఎవరికీ" (these are often
  single tokens; drop the word only if the sentence survives).
- **Immediate repetitions:** "Success and money… Success and money ఈ రెండు language" keep once;
  "తొందరగా result వచ్చేయాలి, తొందరగా result వచ్చేయాలి" keep the rhythm if it's deliberate emphasis.
- **Dead air** longer than about a second, unless it's a dramatic pause before a punch.
- **Tangents:** a name-drop of another guest, a detour into logistics ("which is 2017–18", SAP levels), the second
  example when the first already landed, a "that's a different story".
- **Host monologue:** trim a 15-second question to the 3–5 seconds that state it. Keep the host's words when they
  name the stakes or react honestly ("Very deep", "అయ్యో") at the right spot.

What must stay:
- the **question or setup** the payoff answers;
- every **referent** the kept text leans on;
- **Telugu sentence endings and negations**: "కాదు", "లేదు", "-కూడదు", "అనుకోవచ్చు". Telugu puts the verdict at
  the end of the sentence; cutting the last word can flip the meaning;
- **qualifiers that change a claim**: "just an example", "subject to correction", "for most people", "if you have
  cholesterol issues". Dropping them turns advice into a false claim.

### 3.3 Stitch only when an editor would
Contiguous is the default. Stitch (jump over >20s) only to:
- join a **question** to its **answer** when the guest took a detour first;
- join a **setup** to a **payoff** said later ("director dominate చేసి producer ని కూర్చోబెట్టేసాడు" 45 minutes after
  the "I'm both director and producer" joke);
- drop a **tangent** in the middle of one story.
Never build a montage of separate points. If the clip needs three topics to work, it's three clips or none.

### 3.4 Cold open (hook-first reorder)
Lift one short line (≤ ~10s) to the front when the story's best line comes late and the start is slow. It must
**intrigue, not confuse**, and the rest of the clip must explain it. Good cold opens: a punchline, a shocking line,
an emotional quote.
- "నేనున్నాను, ఏదైనా అమ్మిస్తాను – మా mother చెప్పింది" before a story about composing a climax while broke.
- "18 ఏళ్ళ వాడు… గుచ్చుకోకుండా ఆ body రాదు" before the story of a 'natural' bodybuilding show.
- "Mutton తింటే cancer వస్తది?" (host's line) before the answer.
Either remove the lifted line from its original spot or let it replay there; both are fine when natural.

### 3.5 Length follows the moment
Quick laughs work at 15–30s ("Ego lifting" joke: ~20s). Myth-busts and tips sit at 30–70s. Stories need 60–100s.
A strong story may go to ~2 minutes. The test is not a number but: *could this lose 20% and keep hook, context and
payoff?* If yes, cut it. Do not pad a 25s moment to 45s.

## 4. What kills a clip

| Killer | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow opener** | "So as I said…", "అంటే ఆ ఇది…", greetings, a 15-second host preamble. | Start at the question's core, or cold-open the best line. |
| **Missing context** | Answer without its question; "ఆయన", "అది", "ఆ సినిమా" never resolved; "as you mentioned". | Pull the setup in (even from 1–2 min earlier), or pick a different start. |
| **Inside references** | "మీ mother అంతా వచ్చారు", "ఇందాక చెప్పినట్టు", jokes about something said earlier in the episode. | Drop the line, or explain it within the clip. If it can't be done, it's not a short. |
| **Mid-thought ending** | Ends on "so…", "because…", a list's first item, or the line *before* the punchline. | Extend to the payoff or pick an earlier natural end. |
| **The wrap-up tail** | After the peak, the guest explains it again, or the host says "Very true sir, and next…". | Cut at the peak. |
| **Jargon wall** | Insulin sensitivity, adenosine receptors, SAP levels without a plain-Telugu translation. | Keep the plain analogy ("డబ్బులకి లెక్క ఉన్నట్టే తిండికి లెక్క"), drop the mechanism. |
| **Rambling middle** | Two examples when one lands, false starts, "అది ఇది అని". | Tighten; if it's still 150s, the moment isn't sharp enough. |
| **Meaning changed by the cut** | A dropped "కాదు", a dropped qualifier, an answer attached to a different question. | Never. This is a reject, not a style note. |
| **Promo, intros, outros, sponsor reads** | "Like చేయండి, share చేయండి", "thank you for coming", the guest's film promo with release date. | Skip. Film promos are only usable when a real story is inside them. |
| **Teaser montage copies** | Many episodes open with a cut-up montage of the best lines (often with music or censor beeps). | Take the moment from the body, where it has full context and clean audio. |
| **Censored / low-quality audio** | `[సెన్సార్డ్]` beeps, crosstalk, low-confidence ASR runs. | Exclude those words; if the payoff is censored, drop the clip. |

## 5. Titles and hooks in natural Telugu/Tenglish

A title is the creator's caption or the burnt-in text on the first frame. It sells the moment in under ~60
characters. Write how Telugu creators actually write: Telugu script with English words where people say them in
English. Not textbook Telugu, not full English.

Patterns that work:
- **Question the viewer is already asking:** "బిర్యానీ unhealthy నా?" · "నాటు కోడి vs broiler – అసలు తేడా ఏంటి?"
  · "Whey protein తాగితే పిల్లలు పుట్టరా?"
- **Quote the best line** (in quotes, shortened): "'Diet coke ఎవడూ health కోసం తాగడు'" · "'అమ్మా ₹2000 కావాలి
  supplement కి'"
- **Number + surprise:** "₹17,800 IT job వదిలి fitness coach అయ్యా" · "180 టన్నుల ఒకే రాయి… 17 ఏళ్ళు"
- **Name + tease:** "Villain గా మారడానికి కారణం మణిరత్నం గారే" · "Nani Paradise body కి 18 నెలలు"
- **Contrast:** "Abroad లో pregnant women కి suggest చేస్తారు… మన దగ్గర?" · "Director నేనే, Producer నేనే… మరి గొడవ ఎవరితో?"
- **Relatable confession:** "Peak career లో కూడా పిల్లల shoes polish చేసేవాణ్ణి"

Rules of thumb:
- One idea per title. Promise only what the clip pays off; bait titles kill retention and trust.
- Prefer spoken forms: "ఏంటి", "ఎందుకు", "చేశా", "తగ్గా", "అసలు", "నిజం ఇదే". Avoid formal words like "గురించిన
  వివరణ", "ఆరోగ్య సూత్రాలు".
- Keep brand, medical and political claims as the speaker's words (quote marks), not our claim.
- Ellipsis "…" and a dash " – " are the natural pause marks in Telugu captions. Use sparingly.
- Don't open with the guest's full name and designation; the face on screen does that. Use the name only when the
  name *is* the hook (a star mentioned inside the story).

**Hook line vs on-screen hook.** The `hook_line` is what is *said* first (verbatim). The `on_screen_hook` is the
text on frame one. They can differ: when the spoken opener is a 4, a sharp caption can make the first second a 5
("Mutton తింటే cancer వస్తది?" over a host question).

**`why_it_works`** names the hook and the payoff concretely:
"Opens on 'బిర్యాని నాకు చాలా ఇష్టం', argues home biryani isn't bad, pays off with a 6–7 kg biryani-burger
challenge with blood reports." Not "engaging content about diet".

## 6. Precision over recall: what goes to the top of the list

The creator reviews the list top-down. Put first:
1. clips with a 5-level hook **and** a 5-level payoff (both ends strong);
2. moments with a laugh, an inside story, or a myth-bust people will argue about;
3. clips where the guest is vivid (acts out, quotes others, specific numbers and names).
Put lower: generic advice, host-heavy clips, soft endings, niche craft talk, and near-duplicates of a stronger
clip (two fan-craze stories from the same guest: keep the punchier one on top).

## 7. Things that are taste, not rules (for `product-lead`)

- **Crude or explicit lines** (e.g. a host quoting a crude fan anecdote): I rate the clip on craft and flag it.
  Whether to cut the line or skip the clip is a creator/brand call.
- **Health, finance and brand claims** from guests: same, flag and let the creator decide. A default policy
  (e.g. always add "consult a doctor") is a product decision.
- **Long-range stitches** (setup and payoff 45 minutes apart): editors do them, but they're rarer and riskier.
  Decide whether the pipeline should propose them or leave them to humans.
- **Host-driven clips** (the host's observation is the best part, the guest just agrees): publishable in my view,
  but some creators only want the guest on screen.
- **Length ceiling for stories**: a great 100–120s story is still a Short for Telugu audiences; some editors would
  split it into parts 1 and 2 instead.
