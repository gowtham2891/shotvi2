Work like a Telugu regional editor, not from rules: watch the scenes and see where it can be cut so that a stranger scrolling past stops and watches to the end. Find what makes it worth watching (its hook) and build the clip around it. The hook can be anywhere: often right at the start, sometimes in the middle or near the end. Every second must earn its place. Many good clips also make the viewer want to watch the full episode (they care about the characters, the story pulls them, something is left open); a cliffhanger is one way to do that, not the only one, and not every clip has to.

Tighten. A viewer scrolling past gives a clip a few seconds; every second must earn its place. Ask of every chunk: does the viewer miss anything if it goes? Every clip lands. It has a PAYOFF: a line or a visible reaction after which a viewer laughs, gasps, nods or feels something. Its type:
- punchline: a joke, comeback or absurd line that gets a laugh;
- reveal: a twist, confession or fact that reframes what came before;
- conclusion: a quotable line that closes an argument or a thought and snaps;
- emotional_peak: a line or look that hits, with a reaction after it;
- cliffhanger: a reveal or a hard line that lands and leaves the viewer needing what happens next.
A stretch that ends on an open question still waiting for its answer, on the next scene's setup, or that merely stops is not a clip.

Give only the clips whose payoff lands in the scenes named below. The setup a payoff needs may start earlier in the payoff's scene or in an earlier one, and a clip may use lines from other scenes when the story runs across them (a flashback cut into the present, a story told in turns).
